# Laya v2: pitch and one-page overview draft

Draft for team review. Fictional demo, Stellar testnet only. No partner, funder, or advisor endorsement is claimed.

## Three-minute pitch outline

1. **Maria's problem.** Maria is a fictional Filipina domestic worker preparing to work in Hong Kong. Before her first paycheque she faces a training invoice, a medical exam and a placement fee. Workers may be pushed to borrow before they can earn.
2. **Sort the bills before paying.** Laya applies a small, published demo rulebook. The medical exam is assigned to the employer, training to the worker, and the placement fee is refused in the demo. Unclear bills need review. The demo price caps are illustrative, not official limits.
3. **Show the flow.** Maria submits two accepted invoices. Each provider confirms its invoice. The employer pays 65 test USDC for the exam as a grant. A mission funder pays 190 test USDC for training as a loan with a 3% flat fee, so Maria owes 195.70. The 1,380 test USDC placement fee is refused on screen.
4. **Repayment and receipt.** Maria sends 400 test USDC through Laya. At her chosen 15% share, 60 goes to the funder and 340 to her family; the outstanding balance becomes 135.70. A public receipt records the payment and invoice fingerprint. The fingerprint proves a matching file is unchanged, not that the original invoice was genuine.
5. **Why Stellar.** The demo uses a shared testnet record and an atomic remittance split. It does not process real money or establish that a real-world invoice is lawful.
6. **What we need to validate.** Would employers pay before departure? Would mission lenders fund these small loans? Who would maintain the rulebook and review disputes? Ask advisors for introductions and feedback, not endorsements.

## One-page overview draft

**Problem.** Migrant domestic workers can face recruitment expenses before earning income, including charges that may not be theirs to pay. They may borrow to cover those bills, while reimbursement and verification can be difficult.

**Solution.** Laya is a testnet prototype for a Philippines-to-Hong Kong domestic-work scenario. It classifies a bill using an illustrative rulebook, asks a provider to confirm the invoice, and routes payment directly to that provider. Employer-paid items use a grant; eligible worker-payable items use a small loan with a capped flat fee. The worker can repay through a share of remittances made through Laya. A public receipt contains the payment record and document fingerprint.

**Demo.** A 65 USDC medical bill is paid by an employer as a grant; a 190 USDC training bill is financed at a 3% flat fee; a 1,380 USDC placement fee is refused. These are fictional examples using test tokens. The rulebook's numerical caps are illustrative.

**Limits and next steps.** This is a prototype, not legal advice. The app applies its demo rules; the contract does not enforce them. A hash does not authenticate an invoice. Employers, lenders and legal reviewers have not been secured as partners. Adoption, regulation, dispute handling and the accuracy of the rules require independent validation before real-world use.

**Source starting points.** The team plan lists the Hong Kong Labour Department's foreign domestic helper guide, Hong Kong Employment Agencies Administration guidance, and PCIJ reporting on Migrasia's 2022 survey. Verify each claim against its primary source before submission.
