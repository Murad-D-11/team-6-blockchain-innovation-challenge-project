- **If I am a non-technical team, can I still create a solution for this Innovation Challenge? If so, what will the deliverables be?**
    
    Yes. Non-technical teams can use AI tools to help them code a working prototype or recruit a technical team member to the team. Because this Innovation Challenge is in partnership with the Stellar Development Foundation, which is a technical foundation, a functional technical deliverable is required.
    
- **How do I deploy on Stellar Testnet?**
    
    Testnet is a free practice version of the Stellar network that runs on play money. Instead of a username and password, you create a digital key (like a wallet), fill it with free test money, and use that key to sign whatever you upload. “Building” means turning your code into a file the network can run. “Deploying” means uploading that file, which gives you a contract ID, like a street address for your app on the network.
    
    Step-by-step instructions: 
    
    **1. Install the tools (one time).** Stellar smart contracts are small programs written in the Rust programming language, and you need only a couple prerequisites: Rust itself (from [rust-lang.org](http://rust-lang.org/)) and the Stellar CLI, a command-line tool that talks to the network. On a Mac, the CLI is `brew install stellar-cli`; Windows uses `winget`. You also need a “target” for compiling contracts; for Rust v1.84.0 or higher, that’s wasm32v1-none: [developers.stellar.org +2](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/smart-contracts/getting-started/setup)
    
    ```
    rustup target add wasm32v1-none
    ```
    
    **2. “Log in”: create your key and get free test money.** This step generates a Testnet-funded identity, so it’s both your login and your wallet: [stellar](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/smart-contracts/getting-started/deploy-to-testnet)
    
    ```
    stellar keys generate alice --network testnet --fund
    ```
    
    “alice” is just a nickname. Any name works.
    
    **3. Build: start a project and compile it.**
    
    ```
    stellar contract init my-project
    cd my-project
    stellar contract build
    ```
    
    This creates a starter project with a sample hello-world contract, and the build produces a WASM file at target/wasm32v1-none/release/hello_world.wasm. That file is your app in a form the network can run. [stellar](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tools/cli/agent-cli/guides/build-and-deploy-contracts)
    
    **4. Deploy: upload it to Testnet.**
    
    ```
    stellar contract deploy --wasm target/wasm32v1-none/release/hello_world.wasm --source alice --network testnet --alias hello_world
    ```
    
    This returns the contract’s ID, starting with a C. That ID is what participants would put in their submission as proof the prototype is live. [stellar](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/smart-contracts/getting-started/deploy-to-testnet)
    
    **5. Check that it works.**
    
    ```
    stellar contract invoke --id hello_world --source alice --network testnet -- hello --to world
    ```
    
    The output should be [“Hello”,”world”], which means the contract is running on Testnet. [stellar](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tools/cli/agent-cli/guides/build-and-deploy-contracts)
    
    **Easier path for beginners:** Scaffold Stellar is the fastest route to a working app on Stellar. It’s a toolkit that bundles a CLI, contract templates, a smart contract registry, and a modern frontend for building full-stack dApps. If teams want a clickable website (the “live URL” option), this gets them there faster than building a frontend from scratch. [stellar](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/smart-contracts/getting-started)
    
    Sources:
    
    - [Getting Started (Stellar Docs)](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/smart-contracts/getting-started)
    - [Setup (Stellar Docs)](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/smart-contracts/getting-started/setup)
    - [Deploy to Testnet (Stellar Docs)](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/smart-contracts/getting-started/deploy-to-testnet)
    - [Build and deploy a contract (Stellar Docs)](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tools/cli/agent-cli/guides/build-and-deploy-contracts)
- **What if I can’t attend a session?**
    
    We highly recommend attending all sessions, as each provides important insights and value for the challenge. However, if you’re unable to attend, it’s fine *as long as most of your team* can participate and all team members are present at the: *Kickoff, Project Showcase and Finals.* 
    
    Please *inform us in advance* if you are unable to attend an event so we can make note of your absence, as attendance is recorded. Email [velocity.events@uwaterloo.ca](mailto:velocity.events@uwaterloo.ca) with all Innovation Challenge inquiries. 
    
- **What if my whole team can’t attend a session?**
    
    If your *entire team is unable to attend* a session, please notify us well in advance by emailing velocity.events@uwaterloo.ca. 
    
    Exceptions can be made for valid reasons. However, it’s *mandatory for at least one team member* to attend key in-person events: *Kick-off, Project Showcase, Finalist Pitch Prep and Finals.* 
    
- **Can Velocity or the partners steal my idea?**
    
    No, Velocity and the partners *do not claim ownership of your ideas or intellectual property (IP).* As part of the University of Waterloo’s IP policy, students retain ownership of the IP they create during the challenge. Velocity’s role is to provide guidance, mentorship, and resources to help you develop your solution, not to claim or use your ideas.
    
    For more details, you can refer to the [University of Waterloo’s IP Policy (Policy 73)](https://uwaterloo.ca/secretariat/policies-procedures-guidelines/policies/policy-73-intellectual-property-rights).
    
- **What if another team is working on a similar solution?**
    
    No problem! Teams are judged on how well their approach is *researched, developed, and presented*. Review the judging criteria and focus on excelling in those areas.
    
    Consider it valuable real-world practice - there’s always a chance someone else has a similar idea, but success often comes down to *how effectively you present and execute* your solution.
    
- **For the finals pitch do we have to talk about a revenue model or can the venture idea be a non-profit?**
    
    You *do not need to present a revenue model,* and your venture can absolutely be a non-profit.
    
    However, we want to see that your solution is *practical, applicable, and scalable* in the real world. Please refer to the judging criteria: *"The judges will look for solutions with the potential for integration with existing systems and processes, as well as the ability to scale to meet the needs of large organizations."*
    
- **How many team members should pitch at the Project Showcase and Final Awards presentations?**
    
    Only *one team member should pitch*. This ensures the presentation is *concise, clear, and free* from distractions. It is also in line with startup industry norms of how pitches are presented. 
    
- **How much content should I have on my final slides when pitching?**
    
    Keep it *minimal* - less is more! Use images, big numbers, and one to two-line statements to avoid distractions and keep the focus on you. Avoid reading off your slides; instead, maintain eye contact with the audience and speak with confidence. Your presence and delivery are what make the biggest impact!
    
- **What should we do if we have an unresponsive or inactive team member?**
    
    Email us immediately at [velocity.events@uwaterloo.ca](mailto:velocity.events@uwaterloo.ca) . We’ll contact the team member directly, giving them *24 hours to respond* and explain. If they *remain unresponsive*, they will no longer be part of the challenge.
    
    If needed, we’ll work to find a replacement team member or combine teams, provided both teams agree and a similar situation has occurred with another team.
    
- **What happens after the Innovation Challenge if I would still like to continue working on my solution?**
    
    Velocity is here to support you beyond the innovation challenge, and the strongest next step for developing your solution is joining the Winter 2027 Cornerstone program. Cornerstone will help you continue building your idea, connect you with experienced mentors and a supportive community, and guide you in validating your solution beyond ring road. Please keep an eye on [**this student page**](https://www.velocityincubator.com/students) for the waitlist and registration for the next Cornerstone program.
