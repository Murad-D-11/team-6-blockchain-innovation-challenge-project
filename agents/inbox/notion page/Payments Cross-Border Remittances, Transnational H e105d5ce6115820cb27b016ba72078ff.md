# Payments: Cross-Border Remittances, Transnational Households, and the Last Mile

**Short description:** 

Hundreds of millions of people earn in one country and support a household in another, and they are underserved at both ends. Sending money home still costs roughly 6 percent on average, double the 3 percent target under SDG 10.c, and further cost hides in the exchange rate. The worst failure comes after the transfer succeeds. The money arrives and the recipient still cannot spend it, because the agent who converts it into local currency is far away, out of float, or offline. What happens after arrival is neglected almost entirely. The receiving household is handed a transfer and little else: no savings, no insurance, no credit, no way to pay a school fee directly. The transfer itself is a commodity heading toward zero margin, so the durable opportunity lies in everything attached to it. The same rails and the same last mile carry institutional money. Governments, humanitarian agencies, NGOs, insurers, and large employers disburse enormous sums to these same households, and weeks routinely pass between an approved payment and money in hand. The technology is proven on both flows. Stellar already carries production remittance volumes with major money transfer operators, and Stellar-powered cash assistance has reached displaced people in Ukraine and across Latin America. This space seeks solutions that shorten the distance between a decision and money in hand, and that treat arrival as a beginning rather than an end. Many participants belong to this market themselves, and that lived understanding is a founder advantage the space deliberately rewards.

**Longer description**

**Looking at:**

1. The full financial life of transnational households, meaning people who earn in one economy and support a household in another
2. Cash-in and cash-out access and agent liquidity in thin, rural, or poorly served corridors, where most value is lost
3. Products for the receiving household and not only the sender: savings, insurance, credit, and bill payment built on cross-border income history
4. Transparent all-in pricing, including cost hidden in the exchange rate, and managing obligations across two currencies
5. Institutional and humanitarian disbursement over the same rails, where governments, agencies, NGOs, insurers, and large employers pay the same households, including cash and voucher assistance in crisis settings
6. Eligibility, enrolment, and beneficiary registries, and delivery across bank, mobile-money, agent, card, and wallet rails
7. Reconciliation, treasury, and FX across currencies; fraud, duplication, and leakage detection; audit, donor reporting, and impact measurement

**Challenges and Focus:**

1. Reducing total remittance cost from about 6% toward the SDG 10.c target of 3%, when much of the margin is concealed in the exchange rate
2. The last mile fails after arrival, as agents run dry, sit too far away, or go offline exactly when demand peaks
3. Delivery must work on a smartphone, a basic phone, or through a cash agent, with or without connectivity
4. On the institutional side, weeks pass between approval and money in hand, with meaningful losses to fraud, error, and intermediary fees
5. Verification must be rigorous without becoming surveillance, and recipient dignity, privacy, and choice must be preserved
6. Institutional buyers bring procurement, security reviews, and long sales cycles

**Key Challenges Facing This Category:**

1. Licensing and compliance in both sending and receiving markets, alongside entrenched incumbents and strong habits
2. The receiving side is the harder half to serve and to monetize, and it is where the impact sits
3. Currency volatility and restrictions on holding foreign currency
4. Local infrastructure, agent liquidity, and regulation differ in every market
5. Long procurement and pilot cycles on the institutional side, with strict legal mandates that constrain design
6. Conditional payments can undermine recipient autonomy, and donor transparency must not endanger recipients
7. Institutional risk tolerance is low and failure is highly visible

***SDG alignment: SDGs 1, 2, 3, 8, 10 (Target 10.c), 17***
