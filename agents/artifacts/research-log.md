## Q1. Who puts up the 190?

- **Best answer today · Partly answered.** There are lenders advertising loans for workers leaving the Philippines. We found candidates to interview, with eligibility or timing obstacles for Maria. A Solstice lender remains unconfirmed.
- **Evidence for.** [Budget Finance](https://budgetfinance.ph/ofw-loan) advertises unsecured loans to first-time and returning overseas workers, up to three times basic salary, repayable over 6–18 installments. [Camalig Bank](https://www.camaligbank.com/ofw-loan) explicitly lists BB Larga for first-time overseas workers. [Cebuana](https://cebuanacares.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/18847492940697-FAQs-Cebuana-Lhuillier-Global) lists a first-timer land-based product and advertises a one-to-two-day process.
- **Evidence against fit.** Budget asks for a co-borrower and requires visa, ticket and exit clearance before release. Camalig's shared requirements list proof of remittance and a co-maker, meaning someone else who signs on the loan. Clarify which apply to a first-time worker. Cebuana lists a ₱40,000 income requirement, an immediate-relative co-borrower and a ₱20,000 loan minimum. [Lenderly](https://lenderly.ph/service/ofw-loan/) also lists ₱40,000 minimum monthly salary and clearance documents. [HK's standard minimum wage for newly signed domestic-helper contracts](https://www.immd.gov.hk/eng/faq/foreign-domestic-helpers.html) is HK$5,220 as of Oct 3, 2026. Screen actual applicants' pay and documentation before treating income thresholds as a match. [OWWA's historical pre-departure loan rules](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/48458) describe a program, but current intake and access for this bill are unconfirmed.
- **What's missing.** A verified worker-paid bill, when it falls due, and a lender that can finance it at that point. Public pages give no verified commitment to pay the provider directly on Solstice's terms.
- **Next check.** Ask an overseas-worker lender whether it would fund a first-time domestic worker's confirmed bill before visa, ticket or exit clearance, at what minimum amount and all-in price, how fast it can release funds, and whether it can pay the provider directly.
- **What we'd change.** If existing lenders only release after the bill is due, test whether employer payment, a scholarship, or provider payment terms solve it earlier. Keep the lender in the demo fictional until a real option is confirmed.

## Q2. Does the lender make enough money?

- **Best answer today · Narrow margin, real economics open.** Full repayment yields 5.70 in fees on 190 advanced. That is the amount available for approving and running the loan, tying up money until repayment, moving funds, and absorbing losses. It is the lender's fee. Solstice's own operating costs are a separate question.
- **Evidence for.** [The proposed demo](../context/pitch-context.md) shows the amount and fee. The simple break-even calculation below describes the math.
- **Evidence against fit.** Even a small amount of unrecovered debt or a few units of work can use up most of the fee. Existing direct products have different fees, minimum sizes, document requirements and repayment methods. Their published fees show different offers. They do not reveal the lender's costs or expected losses on Solstice's proposed loan.
- **What's missing.** Approval and servicing cost per loan, cost of capital, actual repayment and recovery data, and costs to convert and transfer funds. The amount recovered from a borrower who falls behind matters. Solstice's model for covering its own costs remains open.
- **Next check.** Ask a lender for the smallest loan it can afford to make, the minimum amount it needs to earn, and the costs or losses that drive that figure. Ask whether a partner pays any of those costs.
- **What we'd change.** If the fee cannot cover real costs, leave 3% as a demo input. Consider a verified source of support or a simpler product. Check pricing and repayment terms with a qualified partner before presenting them as a real offer.

### Example break-even math

These are hypothetical scenarios in test units, with full repayment or zero recovery. They exclude the time value of money and any costs beyond the example cost column. If the lender spends C per loan and a fraction p repay in full, the average amount left is p × 195.70 − 190 − C.

| Example cost per loan | Average left if everybody repays | Share who must repay in full to break even |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 5.70 | 97.09% |
| 2 | 3.70 | 98.11% |
| 5 | 0.70 | 99.64% |
| 6 | −0.30 | Break-even impossible at this fee |

With no other costs, one loan that returns zero wipes out the 5.70 fees from about 33 fully repaid loans. Partial recovery changes the calculation. These figures give the team questions to ask. They estimate neither real costs nor default rates.

## Q3. Why would Maria use Solstice instead of going direct?

- **Best answer today · Partly answered.** The possible benefit is help at the moment the bill arrives. Check who owes it, then make a viable offer available before it is due. If the direct option does that just as well, Maria should take it.
- **Evidence for.** [ICIJ's corridor reporting](https://www.icij.org/investigations/trafficking-inc/philippine-lenders-and-labor-agents-fleece-workers-seeking-overseas-jobs-interviews-and-confidential-documents-show/) documents a Philippines-to-Hong Kong case in which a recruiter sent a worker to a high-cost lender. [ILO/PSA's 2019 survey analysis](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/2024-05/measuring-sustainable-development-goal-indicator-philippines-report-2023-en.pdf) puts recruitment costs at roughly two months of earnings for Filipino domestic-services workers in Hong Kong. Its executive summary says 1.8 and Table 26 gives 1.9, so use the rounded phrasing and date.
- **Evidence against needing the app.** In [Migrasia's mixed-destination survey](https://winrock.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Asia-CTIP-Evaluating-Information-Arbitrage-and-Financial-Exploitation-by-Migration-Intermediaries.pdf), family and friends were the most frequently reported borrowing source in the multiple-answer question. It is not a corridor-specific estimate. [Fair Employment Agency](https://fairagency.org/helper-full-hire/) lists an employer-paid Philippines package that includes medical and visa processing and the TESDA assessment fee for first-time overseas workers. This could remove the need for financing that assessment for workers with access. A worker could also qualify for a direct loan. Compare these options before assuming an expensive lender is the default.
- **What's missing.** A recent case showing a valid worker-paid bill, the bill deadline, which alternative offers were reachable before the deadline, and the exact step Solstice could improve. [DMW rules §§55–58](https://dmw.gov.ph/archives/v1/resources/dsms/DMW/ISN-EXT/2023/DMW-DC-01-2023.pdf) distinguish a competency certificate cost from employer-required additional testing. The fictional school bill does not establish that paid training is required or worker-payable.
- **Next check.** Ask a worker or caseworker to walk through a recent bill. Record who issued it, who owed it, when it had to be paid, when documents became available, and how the worker actually paid. Ask a fair recruiter which bills still fall on the worker even with employer-paid placement.
- **What we'd change.** If bill sorting helps but borrowing doesn't, lead with getting the right party to pay. If the recurring issue is an otherwise eligible bill due before lending documents arrive, test a channel that can act early enough.

## Q4. Why Stellar?

- **Best answer today · Mechanism in code, user benefit open.** One Stellar smart contract call can send the lender and family their shares and update the loan balance. An outside party can independently check the transaction on the network.
- **Evidence for.** [Contract](../../contracts/laya/src/lib.rs) shows worker authorization, both transfers and the balance update. Stellar's [transaction lookup](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/data/apis/rpc/api-reference/methods/getTransaction) lets another party look up recent results. The [receipt page](../../app/src/app/proof/%5Bid%5D/page.tsx) links to the contract. Capture actual payment transaction links for the demo.
- **Evidence against uniqueness.** [Wise](https://wise.com/hk/send-money/send-money-to-philippines) already moves money on this corridor. [Stripe Connect](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/separate-charges-and-transfers) shows conventional payment platforms can distribute a payment among recipients. Its documentation does not establish a ready-made product for this particular loan and corridor. Stellar's network capability alone does not establish cheaper cash delivery, compliance, or a better experience for Maria.
- **What's missing.** A live run with an outsider checking both transfers and the new balance. A worker, lender, or partner who needs a shared record they can verify without Solstice. A comparison of total cost, time, and setup for converting and delivering real money.
- **Next check.** Record the 190 provider payment and the 400 transfer showing 60 to the lender, 340 to family, and 135.70 still owed. Have another teammate verify the transaction in a clean browser. Test a failed transfer leg and confirm neither payment nor the balance update goes through.
- **What we'd change.** Claim the demonstrated payment split and independently checkable transaction once verified. Treat the broader advantage over existing payment services as an open question.
