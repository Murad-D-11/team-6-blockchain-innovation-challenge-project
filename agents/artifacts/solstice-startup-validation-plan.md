# Solstice startup validation plan

**Working hypothesis.** A licensed lender serving Filipino overseas workers would pay Solstice a per-loan servicing fee if provider confirmation, direct payment, and automatic repayment posting make a small pre-departure advance economical. Start with Filipino domestic workers taking jobs in Hong Kong and one verified worker-payable bill. Route employer obligations to the employer before considering credit. The prototype demonstrates the workflow with test funds. Demand, pricing, real payout, and partner commitments are open. [Pitch foundations](../context/solstice-pitch-foundations.md) · [Market evidence](../context/solstice-pitch-evidence.md)

## Before submission

- **Video owner.** Show the employer-paid exam, provider funding, and confirmed repayment split. Give the resulting balance a readable hold. Limit app changes to defects or ambiguity that keep those outcomes from being understood. [One-minute script](solstice-video-script.md)
- **Pitch owner.** Give Devpost one short paragraph naming the lender as the prospective buyer, the work Solstice could save, and the proposed lender-paid fee. Describe the demo’s 3% as an illustrative lender charge to the worker. Use the existing dated evidence and Testnet record. [Devpost draft](solstice-devpost-draft.md)

Have one person outside the team watch once on mute. Ask who owed the exam, who got paid for training, and where the 400 went. Fix a caption or screen only if a viewer misses one of those outcomes, then retest. Keep this independent of partner validation.

## First validation sprint

| Loop | Owner and evidence to collect | Advance or change direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Replay a real bill | Research owner asks a known advisor or caseworker for a recent anonymized timeline. Record who owed the bill, due date, who actually paid, what the worker fronted or borrowed, and what the employer, agency, family, and existing lenders could offer by that date. Replay the same case with Solstice. | Continue toward lending if a verified worker-payable bill can plausibly be paid earlier or at lower all-in cost. If the unresolved bills mainly belong to employers, test employer or agency payment coordination on its own. One case warrants another test. Recurrence needs more cases. |
| 2. Walk through lender operations | Partnerships owner takes the case to an operations lead and a credit or risk owner at a licensed lender. Map provider confirmation, disbursement, repayment posting, and exception handling in their current flow. Ask what would let them fund by the due date, who owns the budget, and whether they would review a priced pilot. | Continue if the lender identifies a step it would change, confirms funding by the needed date is plausible, and names someone to review a priced pilot. If underwriting or release paperwork still blocks funding, cheaper servicing alone leaves the timing gap open. |
| 3. Test repayment and price | Product owner runs a comprehension test with a willing participant through the team’s network. Ask them to predict the family payout, lender payment, and balance before confirming. Partnerships owner compares the same pesos reaching the family and the same amount credited to the loan across both workflows. Include FX, payout fees, failed transfers, missed months, and use of another app. | Revise and retest if the participant misreads the split. Proceed toward a pilot only if there is a plausible positive margin and the worker still gets a better funding outcome without a worse family payout. A usability result establishes comprehension. Actual use and repayment need separate evidence. |

**Price test.** Estimate the lender’s ceiling from observed minutes saved × loaded staff cost, plus documented payment and reconciliation savings, less its added integration and exception costs. Count lower credit losses only if measured. Estimate Solstice’s floor from per-loan delivery costs and the pilot’s share of integration, support, and exceptions. Test a fee above the floor and below what the lender is willing to pay. Include a downside case with low volume and off-platform repayments. Underwriting, identity checks, and compliance stay with the responsible partners.

Start with one invoice timeline and try to find two more before treating it as a recurring problem. Use a synthetic invoice to time the lender walkthrough. Include a duplicate bill and an employer refusal alongside the repayment exceptions. Use a structured invoice reference and provider confirmation first. Revisit PDF extraction if the walkthroughs identify manual entry as a recurring bottleneck.

## How to run each loop

1. Have one subagent assemble the evidence and baseline, one challenge the case against existing alternatives, and one turn the strongest objection into the next test. The lead keeps one shared decision record with the hypothesis, source, result, and next decision.
2. Run the checks available from the repo and public evidence while arranging human validation through the team. Use anonymized case summaries and test funds. Keep the standing constraint against cold outreach to domestic workers. Have the team handle introductions and get permission before sharing any participant information.
3. After each result, update the case and repeat only where a new observation could change the decision. Mark conclusions as observed, estimated, or untested. After two desk-research passes without new evidence, record the missing input and who can supply it, then move to another open test.
4. End the first sprint with one decision. **Pilot** if the worker benefit, buyer, feasible deadline, and economics hold up. **Change direction** if the evidence favors employer payment coordination or a different buyer. **Pause the lending thesis** if existing options already solve the real cases or lenders cannot fund by the bill deadline. Keep any pilot scoped to one provider, one bill type, and one licensed lender, with a remittance partner if needed. Confirm responsibilities and terms before handling real money.
