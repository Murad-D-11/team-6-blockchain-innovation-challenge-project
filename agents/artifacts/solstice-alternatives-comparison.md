# Solstice Alternatives Comparison

Pitch prep draft · Based on the old Laya V2 proposal.

Maria has a bill due before her first paycheck. Later, she’ll send money home. What would make her choose Solstice?

| Option | What it does well | Where it can fall short | What Solstice actually adds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ask the agency to arrange employer payment** | The agency may already have the bill and know how to reach the employer. If the employer pays the clinic directly, Maria borrows nothing. | If the employer pays her back later, she still needs money today. | It shows the employer the clinic bill and gives them a way to pay the clinic directly. The employer still has to agree. If the agency already does this, there is little to add. |
| **Ask a worker-support group** | Someone outside the agency can check who should pay and help Maria question a charge. A call or email may be enough. | Someone still has to agree to pay. | The app can flag a charge using preset rules and block financing it through Solstice. Someone still has to review or challenge the charge. The app has yet to show an advantage over a call or email. |
| **Borrow directly and send money home the usual way** | For a bill Maria owes, she can compare loan offers and keep the service her family knows. | A lender may ask for travel papers she won’t have by the time the bill is due. Paying back the loan may be a separate step. | The school confirms the bill and a lender pays the school directly. Maria can repay a share when she sends money home. Earlier funding or a lower price requires a lender to offer it. |
| **Use Solstice with a regular payment service** | A payment service can split money between family and lender. Solstice can track what Maria still owes. | Each party may rely on the service’s receipts or Solstice’s records. The service has to be able to send money to both the family and the lender. | Solstice could offer the same split with a regular service. Its receipts may be enough for everyone involved. |
| **Use Solstice with Stellar** | In the demo, Maria sends 400 in test money. Her family gets 340, her lender gets 60, and she owes 60 less. The lender can check that record without asking Solstice. | Both payments happen together only when Maria sends through Solstice. We still need to know what it costs and how her family would receive pesos. | The lender can look up the two transfers and check what Maria still owes on Stellar. The test-money record leaves real peso delivery to be confirmed separately. |

**What to show a judge:** For the same bill, compare when it gets paid and what Maria pays in total. For the same amount of pesos her family gets, compare what it costs her to send. Then show what the lender can check for itself in the Stellar demo. Real loan terms, payment costs, and who would offer the service still need testing.

## Could Maria pay a ₱5,000 bill by Friday?

**Fictional test case.** Maria is preparing for her first domestic-worker job in Hong Kong. She has a signed contract but no visa, Overseas Employment Certificate (OEC), or ticket yet. Assume the ₱5,000 bill is genuinely hers and due Friday. Her savings, family support, government contribution history and any offers in her apps are unknown. The public terms below are a first check, with approval and delivery by Friday still unverified.

| Option | Could it cover this bill? | Cost or condition to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Employer or agency payment** | Check who actually owes the bill first. If it is already covered, Maria may avoid borrowing. For the assumed worker-paid bill, ask whether the employer offers help or the provider can move the deadline. | No promise of coverage or an extension. Compare the actual invoice against the hiring package. [Fair Employment Agency](https://fairagency.org/helper-full-hire/) |
| **Savings or family help** | Could cover the exact amount by Friday if the money is available. Enrich's first-job survey documents workers borrowing from family or friends for migration costs. | Ask what Maria could actually raise and when. Include repayment expectations. [Enrich survey](https://enrichhk.org/id/press-release-survey-finds-nearly-30-migrant-domestic-workers-pay-1-3-months-salary-get-their-first) |
| **BDO Network Bank Kabayan** | Published minimum is ₱30,000, and the page lists three years of continuous employment for domestic workers. Poor fit for a first-time worker seeking ₱5,000. | No usable quote for this case. [BDO Network Bank](https://www.bdo.com.ph/bdonetworkbank/loans/kabayan) |
| **Camalig Bank BB Larga** | Advertised for first-time overseas workers. The shared checklist asks for a passport with visa and a co-maker. The page leaves product-specific exceptions unclear. | With no visa, the published checklist is an obstacle. Confirm minimum, total repayment and release time. [Camalig Bank](https://www.camaligbank.com/ofw-loan) |
| **Budget Finance OFW loan** | Accepts applications from first-timers but requires the visa, OEC and ticket before release. Maria is missing all three. | Minimum and total repayment for a ₱5,000 request are unknown from the product page. [Budget Finance](https://budgetfinance.ph/ofw-loan) |
| **Cebuana Lhuillier Global** | Lists first-timers, but its ₱20,000 minimum is four times the bill. It also lists a ₱40,000 income requirement and an immediate-relative co-borrower. | Advertises 1–2 days for processing. The FAQ lists a 5.5% processing fee and 1.99% interest without a clear period in that answer. The total cost needs a quote. [Cebuana FAQ](https://cebuanacares.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/18847492940697-FAQs-Cebuana-Lhuillier-Global) |
| **GCash GLoan** | Strong counterexample if Maria already has a suitable offer. GCash says approved funds reach the wallet within 24 hours. Its eligibility checklist uses account and ID requirements. | Published fee is 3–5% deducted upfront, so a ₱5,000 loan nets only ₱4,750–₱4,850 before other costs. Check the amount actually offered and the full repayment schedule. [Eligibility](https://help.gcash.com/hc/en-us/articles/900006974863-How-to-unlock-and-apply-for-GLoan) · [Timing](https://help.gcash.com/hc/en-us/articles/47623917516825-What-is-GLoan) · [Fees](https://help.gcash.com/hc/en-us/articles/6145865592985-GLoan-fees-and-interest-rate) |
| **Maya Bank Easy Credit** | A bank alternative for this size if Maria already qualifies and has at least ₱5,000 available. Its published eligibility does not list overseas employment documents. | Check the actual fee, how she can pay the provider, and whether repayment would come due before her first paycheck. [Maya Bank](https://www.mayabank.ph/credit/) |
| **Tala** | A general cash loan with a published size range that includes ₱5,000. Tala lists a valid ID for the application. | The amount, price and cash-out timing depend on her actual offer. [Tala application](https://tala.ph/3-easy-steps-to-get-a-tala-loan/) · [Terms](https://tala.ph/user-agreement/) |
| **SSS salary loan or Pag-IBIG Multi-Purpose Loan** | Existing contributions may open another path. SSS generally needs 36 posted contributions for a one-month loan. The Pag-IBIG form lists 24 monthly savings or their equivalent. First overseas job alone tells us little about prior contributions. | Check membership, take-home amount after deductions, approval and release date. [SSS](https://www.sss.gov.ph/salary-loan/) · [Pag-IBIG form](https://www.pagibigfund.gov.ph/document/pdf/dlforms/providentrelated/SLF065_MultiPurposeLoanApplicationForm_V07.pdf) |
| **SSS LoanLite through UnionDigital Bank** | A small loan for eligible SSS members. The published range is ₱1,000–₱20,000, with at least 12 posted contributions required. | SSS lists 15–90 day terms. Check the actual offer and whether funds would arrive before Friday and repayment would fall after her first paycheck. [SSS LoanLite](https://www.sss.gov.ph/sss-loanlite/) · [UnionDigital launch](https://www.sss.gov.ph/news-and-updates/sss-loanlite-now-available-through-uniondigital-bank-mobile-app/) |
| **Solstice** | The proposal lets a lender pay the provider directly. No confirmed lender can fund this example by Friday. | Compare the same ₱5,000 paid by the same deadline and the same amount reaching family later. The demo fee and repayment split cannot be treated as a real quote. |

**Finding.** The checked overseas-worker loans have timing or size barriers for this scenario. Small app-based credit, member loans and family help could still cover it. The case for Solstice is strongest if a real bill falls through those options and a lender can pay it sooner or on terms Maria prefers. The paper case cannot establish how often that happens. Use the advisor to check the bill, deadline and actual alternatives before making it a pitch claim.
