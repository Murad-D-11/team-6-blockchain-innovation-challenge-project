# Solstice pitch foundations

**One line.** Solstice helps Filipino domestic workers get pre-departure bills paid by the right party and repay eligible loans as they send money home.

| | What to communicate |
| --- | --- |
| **Problem** | A worker preparing to move from the Philippines to Hong Kong can face bills before her first paycheck. Some costs belong to the employer, some may legitimately fall to the worker, and some charges should be challenged. Borrowing against the whole stack can saddle her with debt for costs she never owed. |
| **Solution** | Check who owes each bill. Route employer expenses to the employer for direct payment, flag prohibited charges, and let a lender pay the provider directly for an eligible worker expense. The worker chooses a share of transfers through Solstice to credit toward the loan. |
| **Insight** | Who owes the bill changes how much credit the worker needs. Resolve that question before the loan is sized. For the remaining legitimate expense, the transfer she already makes to support her family gives the lender a possible way to collect repayment in the same transaction. |

**Why someone would use it.** A worker could front less cash and take on less principal. A lender could fund a specific verified bill and receive repayment when the worker sends money through the service. The growth hypothesis is that a lender and remittance partner could offer these small advances economically if verification and repayment take less work per loan. That depends on partner costs, repayment behavior, and whether workers will use the transfer service. [Kevin Hale on problem, solution, and insight](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6q-how-to-pitch-your-startup) · [Evidence and limits](solstice-pitch-evidence.md)

## What to put on screen

- **Make the principal decision visible.** Show the sample clinic bill routed to the employer, the Philippine recruiter placement fee refused by the app, and only the hypothetical training invoice funded by the lender.
- **Make the repayment visible.** Of 400 test USDC sent, 340 goes to family and 60 to the lender. The balance falls from 195.70 to 135.70. Stellar executes the transfers and updates the loan balance in one transaction. The team recorded this for Testnet loan #13 and a separate employer clinic payment for request #14. The worker, invoices, and loan terms are illustrative. [Demo and transaction records](../artifacts/solstice-video-script.md)

The mock smoke run reproduced the amounts during this review. A fresh Testnet read could not load configuration in this environment. Use the recorded run for Testnet evidence. Real currency payout and lender economics still need verification. ## What would change our confidence

1. Replay a recent anonymized case with a caseworker or advisor. For each bill, record who owed it, who actually paid, when it was due, and what the worker could already use. See whether Solstice would reduce worker cash paid before departure or principal borrowed. An employer already paying through an agency can erase the benefit for that bill.
2. Have a non-team participant predict the family payout, lender payment, and remaining balance before confirming the transfer. Record any confusion, revise the screen, and retest. This checks comprehension. A willing sender from the team's network could also compare the flow with their current transfer routine.
3. Ask a prospective lender and remittance partner to price the same loan and the same family payout under the current process and the proposed one. Check all-in cost, settlement and servicing effort, and what happens when a worker sends nothing or uses another service. Partner willingness, savings, and terms remain hypotheses.
